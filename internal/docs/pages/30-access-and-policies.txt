Access and policies

A policy is a name and a list of rules. Admins assign policies to teams, and to people without a team directly. You get every rule of every policy that reaches you.

Rules only give access; no rule takes it away. To give access to everything under /apps except /apps/payments, list the parts you do want instead: /apps/search/* and /apps/web/*. Keeping items that need different access under different prefixes makes this easy.

A rule applies to SSM parameters or to secrets, has a pattern, and grants permissions.

Patterns
An exact name, like /payments/api/key, or a prefix ending in *, like /payments/*, which covers everything below it, at any depth. A * is only allowed at the end. A pattern of just * covers everything.

Permissions
list: see the item in lists and search, and open its page. Never the value.
create: create new items with a matching name. Never overwrites.
edit: change the value, description and note, and roll back. Includes list.
reveal: see the current and earlier values. Includes list.

Items you can't list don't exist for you: they never show up in lists or search, and opening one by name says it doesn't exist.

Teams from identity-provider groups
A team can be linked to one group of your identity provider (Okta, Entra ID, Dex, ...). Its members then come from that group: whoever is in the group is in the team, and their team's policies reach them. Nothing else changes; platypus never writes to the identity provider.

In oidc and signed-header mode platypus reads the groups from the login token, in header mode from a header the login proxy sets. All three are off unless the deployment switches them on; ask whoever runs your platypus whether groups are in use.

When platypus sees your login it puts you in the linked teams your groups name and takes you out of the others. In oidc mode that happens at each login: leave a group, and after your next login the team is gone. Teams without a group link keep their manually chosen members.

Things to know:
- Whoever manages groups in the identity provider decides who is on a linked team, and so who gets its policies. Only link groups managed by people you would trust with those policies.
- A group feeds at most one team. Several teams for the same people? Give one team several policies.
- Linked teams show their members but don't let admins add or remove them; that happens in the identity provider.

Admins
Admins are people in the admin group. They use platypus like everyone else and switch on admin mode with a second factor when they need to manage it. Admin mode doesn't give access to values; that only comes from policies, for admins too.

Hidden items
Platypus hides items that it can't access itself in AWS, for example a secret whose resource policy denies platypus. Admins can ask the worker to check access again from the admin overview.
